Technical note Why the CDF of a B-spline mixture has cumulative-sum coefficients Degree elevation turns the mixture CDF into a higher-degree spline. The normalization constants cancel, so the coefficients are cumulative sums of the mixture weights and the integral does not have to be computed numerically.
Consider a B-spline basis of degree \(\rho\) on a clamped knot vector
\[t_1\leq\cdots\leq t_{K+\rho+1}.\]
We normalize each basis function \(B_{k,\rho}\) to obtain a probability density:
\[b_k(e)=\frac{B_{k,\rho}(e)}{a_k}, \qquad a_k=\frac{t_{k+\rho+1}-t_k}{\rho+1}.\]
For weights \(\gamma\) on the probability simplex, the mixture is
\[q_\gamma(e)=\sum_{k=1}^K\gamma_k b_k(e).\]
The objective is to represent
\[F_\gamma(e)=\int_0^e q_\gamma(s)\,ds\]
as another spline, without performing numerical integration. ### The derivative identity A standard B-spline derivative identity is
\[\frac{d}{de}B_{k,\rho+1}(e) = \frac{\rho+1}{t_{k+\rho+1}-t_k} B_{k,\rho}(e) - \frac{\rho+1}{t_{k+\rho+2}-t_{k+1}} B_{k+1,\rho}(e),\]
with indices interpreted on the degree-raised, boundary-extended knot vector. Suppose we express the CDF as
\[F_\gamma(e)= \sum_{m=0}^K c_m\bar B_{m,\rho+1}(e).\]
Differentiating the expansion and collecting the coefficients of the degree-\(\rho\) basis gives
\[F_\gamma'(e) = \sum_{k=1}^K \frac{c_k-c_{k-1}}{a_k} B_{k,\rho}(e).\]
But the derivative of the CDF must equal the original density:
\[F_\gamma'(e) = q_\gamma(e) = \sum_{k=1}^K \frac{\gamma_k}{a_k}B_{k,\rho}(e).\]
Matching coefficients therefore gives
\[c_k-c_{k-1}=\gamma_k.\]
Since \(F_\gamma(0)=0\), the initial coefficient is \(c_0=0\), and hence
\[c_m=\sum_{k=1}^m\gamma_k.\]
Thus
\[\boxed{ F_\gamma(e)= \sum_{m=0}^K \left(\sum_{k=1}^m\gamma_k\right) \bar B_{m,\rho+1}(e) }\]
with the coefficient at \(m=0\) equal to zero. The final coefficient is
\[c_K=\sum_{k=1}^K\gamma_k=1,\]
which also ensures \(F_\gamma(1)=1\). The cancellation of the normalization constants \(a_k\) is the crucial feature. For an ordinary spline expansion, antiderivative coefficients require knot-dependent scaling. For a mixture of normalized B-splines, the coefficients are simply cumulative probabilities. This is what makes inverse-CDF sampling especially convenient for this family.
