**The obstacle.** In original SVI, natural gradients are simple for a minimal exponential family. A mixture approximation is richer, but its Fisher matrix is harder to manipulate.

**Mathematical move.** A mixture \(q_\phi(z)=\sum_{k=1}^K\omega_kq_{\phi_k}(z)\) introduces a latent component label. Representing the enlarged construction through a suitable minimal conditional exponential-family formulation allows practical natural-gradient updates without naïvely inverting a huge Fisher matrix. The fundamental identity remains \(\widetilde\nabla_\phi\mathcal L=F(\phi)^{-1}\nabla_\phi\mathcal L\), but the representation controls the computational cost of applying it.

**Reusable insight.** **Geometry depends on parameterization**. A complex mixture may admit simple updates after a judicious representation change. This unites family design and optimization rather than treating them independently.

A component label tells the optimizer which local exponential-family geometry to use. The tractable Fisher structure belongs to that joint conditional representation; it should not be confused with blindly inverting the Fisher matrix of the marginal mixture. Richer families can capture multiple modes, but component initialization and stochastic-gradient noise still affect which modes are found.
