ARM already couples its two binary samples. Yet after the two binary outcomes have been determined, the exact uniform numbers used to create them still vary. DisARM notices that this leftover variation can be integrated out analytically, preserving the same mean with no extra function evaluations.

The paired discrete outcomes in ARM usually leave some uncertainty about the uniforms that produced them. DisARM analytically computes \(\mathbb E[\widehat g_{\phi,\mathrm{ARM}}\mid z^+,z^-]\). By total variance,

\[
\operatorname{Var}\big(\mathbb E[\widehat g_{\phi,\mathrm{ARM}}\mid z^+,z^-]\big)
\leq \operatorname{Var}(\widehat g_{\phi,\mathrm{ARM}}).
\]

This is a direct improvement to [ARM](#paper-20) by Rao–Blackwellization. It keeps the paired function evaluations and integrates out the unnecessary randomness between the thresholds.

**Return to ARM's fair-coin example.** When \(p=1/2\), knowing which of the two outcomes occurred tells us whether \(u\) lies in the lower or upper half of the unit interval. ARM's value still depends on the precise location within that half. But its conditional average is already known:

\[
\mathbb E[|u-\tfrac12|\mid z^+,z^-]=\tfrac14.
\]

For one bit and a fixed \(f\), DisARM therefore returns the exact derivative \(\tfrac14[f(1)-f(0)]\) in this example. The function values are unchanged; we have integrated out a random multiplier that was no longer needed.

More generally, the paper derives the conditional expectations associated with the paired binary outcomes. Total variance splits ARM's variability into the variability of these conditional means and the remaining variability within each condition. DisARM removes the second part, explaining the variance improvement directly rather than relying only on an empirical comparison.

**What remains random.** In a multivariate problem, the paired configurations still vary, and a nonlinear \(f\) can assign them very different values. Conditioning does not enumerate all configurations or solve their interactions. The gain is relative to the corresponding ARM construction; it does not establish that DisARM is cheaper or lower-variance than every learned control-variate method on every model.

**What carries forward.** This is Rao–Blackwellization applied to an estimator rather than to a posterior approximation. Whenever an estimator contains auxiliary randomness that can be conditioned away exactly, this is a general route to variance reduction.
