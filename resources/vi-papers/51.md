[CAVI](#paper-1) gives us a factor update, but deriving a separate implementation for every model is tedious. Variational message passing turns that update into a reusable computation on a graph.

Suppose the model has local factors \(\gamma(z)=\prod_a\psi_a(z_a)\), where \(z_a\) contains the variables used by factor \(a\). Keep the mean-field approximation \(q_\phi(z)=\prod_jq_j(z_j)\). Only factors touching \(z_j\) can affect its update:

\[
\log q_j^\star(z_j)
=\sum_{a:j\in a}\underbrace{\mathbb E_{q_{-j}}[\log\psi_a(z_a)]}_{\text{message from factor }a}
+c_j.
\]

Here \(c_j\) makes the density integrate to one. The expectation averages the other variables in each neighboring factor. A distant factor contributes nothing that varies with \(z_j\).

**The graph tells us who needs to communicate.** In a chain with factors \(\psi_{12}(z_1,z_2)\) and \(\psi_{23}(z_2,z_3)\), updating \(q_2\) combines two messages. Updating \(q_1\) only needs the first. Information still travels farther over successive updates, because the neighboring distributions themselves change.

Conjugate-exponential structure makes these messages compact. If the terms depending on \(z_j\) are linear in sufficient statistics \(T_j(z_j)\), their coefficient vectors add:

\[
q_j^\star(z_j)\propto h_j(z_j)
\exp\!\left\{T_j(z_j)^\top
\sum_{a:j\in a}\mathbb E_{q_{-j}}[\lambda_{a\to j}]\right\}.
\]

The \(\lambda_{a\to j}\) are the natural-parameter contributions of neighboring factors; \(h_j\) is the base measure. Software can implement these local rules once and reuse them across models.

**What changes.** The ELBO and mean-field family stay in place. Model factorization localizes the computation; conjugacy turns it into moment and natural-parameter messages. Exact sequential factor updates retain CAVI's ascent property.

This is useful for assembling large models from familiar probabilistic components. It does not restore dependence removed by mean field. Nonconjugate factors need additional approximations or bounds; see [nonconjugate VI](#paper-53). [Expectation propagation](#paper-65) also passes local information, but uses a different projection.
