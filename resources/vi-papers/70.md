**The obstacle.** A mean-field approximation may introduce unnecessary independence between variables that are analytically integrable.

**Mathematical move.** Consider a joint target \(\gamma(z,a)\) with latent assignments \(z\) and nuisance variables \(a\). Integrate out the nuisance variables to form \(\gamma_{\rm col}(z)=\int\gamma(z,a)\,da\), then approximate the smaller target. In LDA, conjugate Dirichlet distributions allow topic proportions and/or topic-word probabilities to be removed, leading to terms built from counts and Gamma functions. A generic Jensen argument explains the gain: optimizing an unrestricted conditional factor \(q(a\mid z)\) for fixed \(q(z)\) reaches the marginal ELBO; restricting it unnecessarily can only weaken the best achievable bound.

**Reusable insight.** **Rao–Blackwellization can happen at the level of the variational objective**, not only inside a gradient estimator. Collapsing often tightens a bound and reduces the number of free parameters, although it can couple the remaining factors.

The distinction can be written exactly. With \(\pi(a\mid z)\) the model's conditional distribution,
\[
\mathcal L[q(z)q(a\mid z)]
=\mathcal L_{\rm col}[q(z)]
-\mathbb E_{q(z)}\mathrm{KL}\bigl(q(a\mid z)\|\pi(a\mid z)\bigr).
\]
Integrating out \(a\) removes the need to approximate that conditional. In LDA, however, the remaining assignment counts interact. The paper uses approximations to their expected log terms to obtain practical updates. A simpler latent space does not necessarily mean simpler coordinate calculations.
