Changing the divergence can encourage greater target coverage. But if we want a particularly strong penalty when the target is large and the proposal is small, we can look at higher moments of importance weights. CHIVI uses those moments to construct an upper bound rather than another lower bound.

For \(n\gt 1\), the power-mean inequality gives

\[
\log Z=\log\mathbb E_q[w]\leq \frac1n\log\mathbb E_q[w^n].
\]

Minimizing this upper bound, called CUBO, is equivalent to minimizing the corresponding powered density-ratio discrepancy from \(\pi\) to \(q\). The algorithm can differentiate \(\mathbb E_q[w^n]\) directly, avoiding bias from estimating the gradient of the logarithm of that expectation by a ratio. Large weights receive more emphasis than under the ELBO, making thin tails of \(q\) consequential. [Tail-adaptive VI](#paper-31) addresses the instability that can arise when these powered weights have heavy tails.

**The case \(n=2\) connects approximation to importance sampling.** Since \(w=Z\pi/q\),

\[
\frac{\mathbb E_q[w^2]}{Z^2}
=\int\frac{\pi(z)^2}{q(z)}\,dz
=1+\chi^2(\pi\|q).
\]

Consequently, minimizing this discrepancy also minimizes the relative variance of an importance-weight estimate of \(Z\). A region with appreciable target mass but very little proposal mass creates enormous ratios and a large penalty. This explains the tendency to cover target mass, including regions a reverse-KL fit might neglect.

The ELBO below and CUBO above can conceptually bracket the log evidence. To optimize CUBO, however, differentiating a finite-sample log average is not equivalent to differentiating the exact log expectation. The paper instead uses the monotonicity of the logarithm and optimizes the powered expectation, for which an unbiased stochastic gradient can be formed.

**What can go wrong.** A narrow proposal can give a divergent second moment or occasional overwhelming weights. In that case the objective is difficult to estimate precisely where its coverage penalty is needed most. Finite Monte Carlo estimates of an upper-bound expression are also not automatically certified upper bounds on \(\log Z\). The method's useful coverage behavior comes with a demand for adequate proposal tails and manageable weight moments.

**What carries forward.** The connection to importance-sampling variance is especially direct at second order. The transferable lesson is that objectives controlling proposal coverage and importance-weight moments are related, but they can also become difficult to estimate when those moments explode.
