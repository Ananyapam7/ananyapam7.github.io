**The obstacle.** Particle methods inherit stochastic-gradient noise and can move slowly in ill-conditioned directions.

**Mathematical move.** Combine variance-reduced estimators with quasi-Newton information in particle-based inference. The conceptual analogy to stochastic optimization is to correct noisy directions using stored/reference information and rescale by curvature, while retaining the interaction that spreads particles. Details of the specific update depend on the particle kernel and the chosen gradient estimator.

**What changes.** **Optimizer and gradient estimation for particles**, rather than model family, reverse-KL definition or posterior objective.

The geometry matters because particle locations represent a distribution rather than independent optimization problems. The paper transfers variance reduction and limited-memory curvature ideas through the Riemannian structure of Wasserstein space. It evaluates posterior sample quality as well as optimization speed. Extra reference-gradient and curvature work must pay for itself; preconditioning cannot compensate for an inadequate particle representation.
