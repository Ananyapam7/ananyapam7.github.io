A semi-implicit distribution is easy to sample but its marginal log density is difficult to evaluate. Kernel SIVI avoids that quantity by changing the discrepancy used to fit the approximation.

Write \(s_\pi(z)=\nabla_z\log\gamma(z)\) for the target score. A Stein operator tests the target through
\[
\mathcal T_\pi f(z)=s_\pi(z)^\top f(z)+\nabla_z\!\cdot f(z).
\]
For suitable test functions, its expectation under the target is zero. Kernel Stein discrepancy measures how strongly the approximation violates this identity:
\[
\operatorname{KSD}(q_\phi,\pi)=\sup_{\|f\|_{\mathcal H}\leq1}\mathbb E_{q_\phi}[\mathcal T_\pi f(z)],
\]
where \(\mathcal H\) is a vector-valued reproducing kernel Hilbert space. The kernel gives the optimal critic analytically, eliminating the separately trained inner critic of score-matching SIVI.

The hierarchy \(u\sim r_\phi,\ z\sim q_\phi(z\mid u)\) makes the resulting objective computable using conditional scores and pairs of samples. Intuitively, the critic searches for a direction in which the sampled distribution disagrees with the target; the kernel solves that search without a second neural-network optimizer.

**What changes.** This is a KSD objective and a tractable optimization construction, not just a new estimator of the original ELBO gradient. Kernel choice, bandwidth, and the geometry of high-dimensional samples affect the signal. The paper gives gradient-variance and stationary-point guarantees under its assumptions; these do not imply every kernel detects every posterior error equally well.
