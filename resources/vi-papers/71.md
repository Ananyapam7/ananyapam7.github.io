**The obstacle.** Alternating CAVI factors can move slowly because one factor reacts to another's outdated value.

**Mathematical move.** For ELBO \(\mathcal L(q_a,q_b)\), define the partially optimized objective \(\mathcal L_{\rm col}(q_a)=\max_{q_b}\mathcal L(q_a,q_b)\). The envelope theorem gives its derivative using the optimum \(q_b^\star(q_a)\), without differentiating through the full optimizer under regularity. The paper derives collapsed bounds for conjugate-exponential graphs and exploits their geometry to use conjugate-gradient optimization rather than only alternating full coordinate steps.

**What changes.** Same probabilistic model and target, but **a partially collapsed optimization landscape and an improved optimizer**. This is a general recipe when some factors can be solved analytically.

Imagine changing one factor while every analytically solvable factor immediately adjusts to it. The collapsed objective follows that response instead of waiting for the next CAVI sweep. This differs from integrating the original model first: the maximization is over the chosen variational factors. The benefit depends on conjugate structure; it is unavailable when that inner optimum cannot be computed.
