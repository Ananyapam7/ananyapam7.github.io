Nonconjugacy prevents analytic updates, but it need not prevent differentiation. Doubly stochastic variational Bayes builds a continuous approximation by transforming simple noise, then differentiates the target at the resulting samples.

Let \(\epsilon\sim\nu\) have a fixed, easy-to-sample density, and set

\[
z=m+B\epsilon,\qquad
q_\phi(z)=|\det B|^{-1}\nu(B^{-1}(z-m)),
\qquad \phi=(m,B).
\]

The scale matrix \(B\) can be lower triangular with positive diagonal entries. The base law need not be Gaussian. The ELBO becomes

\[
\mathcal L(\phi)
=\mathbb E_{\epsilon\sim\nu}[\log\gamma(m+B\epsilon)]
+\log|\det B|+H(\nu).
\]

**Move the samples and observe how the target responds.** For the mean,
\[
\nabla_m\mathcal L
=\mathbb E_\nu[\nabla_z\log\gamma(m+B\epsilon)].
\]
Thus each draw provides an ordinary target gradient. The scale gradient also differentiates the affine map and the log determinant. Conjugacy is unnecessary because the expectation is estimated rather than integrated analytically.

For factorized likelihoods, \(\gamma(z)=p_0(z)\prod_i p(x_i\mid z)\), subsampling data adds a second random approximation:

\[
\widehat{\mathcal L}
=\log p_0(z)
+\frac{N}{|\mathcal B|}\sum_{i\in\mathcal B}\log p(x_i\mid z)
+\log|\det B|+H(\nu),
\qquad z=m+B\epsilon.
\]

With a uniformly sampled minibatch, averaging over both \(\epsilon\) and \(\mathcal B\) recovers the ELBO. One randomness approximates an expectation over latent variables; the other approximates a sum over observations.

**What changes.** This is a location-scale pathwise optimization method, closely related to [AEVB](#paper-4), applied to nonconjugate Bayesian inference. It does not require an amortized encoder, nor the conjugate global–local structure used for the particular natural-gradient updates in [SVI](#paper-2).

It is useful when target derivatives are available and a correlated location-scale approximation is adequate. Its shape remains constrained by the chosen base distribution. A single affine transformation cannot turn a unimodal Gaussian into separated modes. Nonsmooth targets, unstable scales, or very noisy minibatches also make optimization difficult. The two sources of stochasticity enable scale, but both contribute gradient noise.
