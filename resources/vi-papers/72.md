**The obstacle.** VI objectives are nonconvex in many models, so CAVI and SVI can become trapped in poor local optima.

**Mathematical move.** A deterministic temperature changes relative likelihood strength, for example replacing a factor \(p_\theta(x\mid z)\) by \(p_\theta(x\mid z)^{1/T}\), with the necessary normalization handled in the chosen construction. At high temperature, the objective can be easier to explore; as temperature approaches one, the original model is recovered. The paper also treats temperature as a latent variable, making the tempering schedule adaptive, and develops local temperature variables for data-dependent annealing.

**What changes.** This is **a continuation strategy or augmented probabilistic model**, not an unbiased gradient estimator for a fixed original ELBO at every temperature. Tempering helps optimization but requires care about which objective is ultimately being targeted.

For a fixed temperature, a useful schematic objective is
\[
\mathcal L_T(\phi)=T^{-1}\mathbb E_{q_\phi}\log p(x\mid z)
-\mathrm{KL}(q_\phi(z)\|p_0(z)),
\]
up to terms constant in \(\phi\). Increasing \(T\) weakens the pull of individual likelihood peaks. The paper's learned-temperature construction must additionally account for the temperature-dependent model normalization; its local version lets observations receive different temperatures. This can help avoid a poor basin, but a learned tempered model need not equal the original \(T=1\) posterior.
