An encoder can easily generate \(z=T_\phi(x,\epsilon)\) while making its density \(q_\phi(z\mid x)\) impossible to evaluate. That blocks the entropy term in the [AEVB](#paper-4) objective. Adversarial variational Bayes learns the density ratio that the ELBO needs.

Train a discriminator \(D_\psi(x,z)\) to distinguish two equally likely sources: pairs drawn from the encoder, and pairs formed using the same data distribution but an independent prior draw. At its ideal optimum,

\[
D^\star(x,z)=\frac{q_\phi(z\mid x)}
{q_\phi(z\mid x)+p(z)}.
\]

Its log odds therefore satisfy
\[
T^\star(x,z)=\log\frac{D^\star(x,z)}{1-D^\star(x,z)}
=\log q_\phi(z\mid x)-\log p(z).
\]

**Classification reveals a ratio without evaluating either source through the classifier.** If a particular pair is four times as likely under the encoder source as under the prior source, the optimal classifier assigns probability \(4/5\) to the encoder label, and its log odds are \(\log4\).

Substitute that ratio into the familiar objective:

\[
\mathcal L(\theta,\phi;x)
=\mathbb E_{\epsilon\sim\nu}
\left[\log p_\theta(x\mid T_\phi(x,\epsilon))
-T^\star(x,T_\phi(x,\epsilon))\right].
\]

As in AEVB, \(\theta\) learns the generative model and \(\phi\) learns the encoder. In practice, discriminator updates alternate with model and encoder updates. The discriminator sees \(x\), so it estimates a conditional ratio, not merely a ratio for the aggregated latent distribution.

**What changes.** The encoder can be an implicit sampler; an auxiliary learning problem supplies the missing log-density ratio. The intended objective remains the ELBO, unlike the operator discrepancy in [Operator VI](#paper-55). [Flows](#paper-34) solve the density problem differently, by retaining an explicit change-of-variables formula.

This is useful when a simple Gaussian encoder is too restrictive. Its cost is an additional approximation: a weak or lagging discriminator gives a wrong penalty and misleading gradients. The exact-ratio identities describe an ideal discriminator, not a guarantee for a finite neural network. Expressiveness alone also does not ensure that adversarial optimization will find the desired fit.
