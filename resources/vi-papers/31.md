Objectives that emphasize large importance weights can encourage coverage, but a few enormous weights can dominate their gradients. Tail-adaptive VI seeks a more stable way to express that preference.

Tail-adaptive VI adapts an \(f\)-divergence through the observed tail behavior of importance weights, aiming for coverage without unstable high weight moments. The proposal family and model can remain fixed, but the discrepancy being optimized changes. This differs from subtracting a zero-mean control variate from a gradient of a fixed ELBO.

A fixed-power divergence can give too much influence to rare, enormous values of \(\pi(z)/q(z)\). The paper constructs a divergence using the tail probability of that ratio rather than applying the same fixed power to its magnitude everywhere. In a sample, tail probabilities can be estimated from the ranks of the importance weights. This retains sensitivity to regions where \(q\) assigns little mass relative to \(\pi\), while controlling moments of the resulting importance factors under the construction. [Taylor controls](#paper-23) and [Control ensembles](#paper-24) and [Learned quadratic controls](#paper-25) and [Joint control variate](#paper-26) modify gradient noise at a fixed objective. Tail-adaptive VI changes how the objective weights discrepancies.

**Coverage and stability can pull in opposite directions.** A large importance ratio points to a region the approximation underrepresents. We would like the optimizer to notice it. But if its influence grows as a fixed power, a single rare draw can overwhelm every other update. Increasing coverage pressure can therefore destroy the quality of the stochastic gradient.

The paper adapts the weighting to the tail of the ratio distribution. In a batch, a useful empirical quantity is

\[
\widehat S(w_s)=\frac1K\sum_{r=1}^K\mathbf1\{w_r\geq w_s\},
\]

the fraction of weights at least as large as \(w_s\). These ranks describe how extreme a draw is relative to the current sample. They are unchanged by multiplying every weight by the same unknown normalizing factor, so the method can work with unnormalized targets.

The divergence construction uses such tail information to retain pressure toward missed mass while controlling the moments of the weights used in its gradient. The discrepancy adapts to the current approximation, rather than selecting one fixed power once and for all.

**Its limits.** Ranks only describe regions actually sampled. If no draw reaches a missing mode, there is no tail signal revealing it. Small batches also give coarse, noisy tail estimates, and ranking loses information about the size of gaps between weights. The finite-batch adaptive update should not be interpreted as an exact gradient of an arbitrary fixed divergence.
