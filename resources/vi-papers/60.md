A Gaussian process couples function values at all \(N\) observations. Exact Gaussian regression is tractable algebraically, but dense covariance operations cost roughly \(N^3\). Titsias uses VI to choose a smaller set of function values through which to represent posterior information.

Let \(f\) be the function values at training inputs, and let \(u\) contain values at \(M\ll N\) inducing inputs. Together, these form the latent vector \(z=(f,u)\). Augmenting the GP with \(u\) preserves the original model. Choose

\[
q_\phi(f,u)=p(f\mid u)q_\phi(u).
\]

**Learn the distribution at a few anchor locations; let the GP conditional fill in the rest.** The approximation keeps dependence between \(f\) and \(u\). Its restriction is that observations influence \(f\) only through \(q_\phi(u)\), rather than through a separately fitted \(q(f\mid u)\).

The matching prior conditional cancels inside the ELBO:

\[
\mathcal L(\phi)
=\mathbb E_{q_\phi(f)}[\log p(y\mid f)]
-\mathrm{KL}(q_\phi(u)\|p(u)).
\]

For zero-mean GP regression with Gaussian observation noise of variance \(\sigma^2\), optimizing out \(q_\phi(u)\) gives

\[
\mathcal L^\star
=\log\mathcal N(y\mid0,Q_{NN}+\sigma^2I)
-\frac{1}{2\sigma^2}\operatorname{tr}(K_{NN}-Q_{NN}),
\qquad Q_{NN}=K_{NM}K_{MM}^{-1}K_{MN}.
\]

The \(K\) blocks are prior covariances between training and inducing locations. The trace measures prior variation at the training points that the inducing values cannot explain. Thus the approximation pays a penalty for information its anchors leave out.

**What changes.** The paper restricts the posterior representation and optimizes inducing locations as variational choices. It does not simply replace the original covariance by a low-rank model. Computation becomes roughly \(O(NM^2)\), with kernel hyperparameters optionally learned through the bound.

Too few inducing values cannot capture rapidly varying functions or complicated dependence. The analytic collapsed expression above uses Gaussian likelihoods; other likelihoods require further expectation estimates. This is a concrete structured approximation, related to [Structured SVI](#paper-52) in preserving dependence, but with a different mechanism and computational bottleneck.
