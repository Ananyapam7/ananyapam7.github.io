**The obstacle.** If uncertainty over individual weights is learned, could it also identify which connections are dispensable?

**Mathematical move.** Use weight-specific multiplicative noise with learned dropout rates in a variational Bayesian formulation. Optimization can drive some weights into very high-noise regimes, making them effectively removable. The ELBO contains a data-fit term and a KL/regularization term; the form of the prior and tractable KL approximations are essential to the sparsification behavior.

**What changes.** Variational **weight-family and prior/regularizer design**, rather than a general new ELBO identity. A high inferred dropout rate becomes a pruning criterion; posterior uncertainty and model compression meet.

For a weight, the family can be written
\[
q_\phi(w_j)=\mathcal N(m_j,\alpha_j m_j^2).
\]
The paper uses an additive-noise parameterization to stabilize gradients when \(\alpha_j\) becomes large. Large noise-to-signal ratios identify weights to prune. Its log-uniform prior is improper, so the resulting KL-shaped term is interpreted as a regularizer rather than a divergence to a normalized prior. This choice is central to the sparsity effect, not a generic property of Bayesian weight uncertainty.
