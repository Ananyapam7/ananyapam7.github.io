**The obstacle.** A multivariate Gaussian has tractable correlation but Gaussian marginals can misrepresent skewness and tails.

**Mathematical move.** Use a Gaussian copula for dependence and flexible one-dimensional transformations for marginals. A representative form is \(z_j=F_j^{-1}(\Phi(y_j))\), with \(y\sim\mathcal N(0,R)\). The matrix \(R\) controls dependence; \(F_j\) controls the marginal shape. The paper uses flexible Bernstein-polynomial-based marginal transformations to approximate non-Gaussian posteriors.

**What changes.** Variational family design, with independent knobs for correlation and marginal behavior. This may be easier to interpret than a generic high-dimensional flow, but is limited by the chosen copula and transforms.

Here \(\Phi\) is the standard normal CDF and \(R\) is a correlation matrix. Transforming each coordinate preserves its rank ordering, so the Gaussian copula retains a particular dependence structure even when the marginals become skewed. Flexible marginal shapes cannot by themselves reproduce every kind of tail dependence or multimodality.
