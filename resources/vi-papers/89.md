**The obstacle.** When each data point contributes its own Monte Carlo estimate, a single generic baseline may ignore substantial observation-specific variation.

**Mathematical move.** Learn a function that predicts useful control-variate parameters from the observation or its context. For estimator \(G(i,\epsilon)\) and centered control \(C(i,\epsilon)\) with \(\mathbb E_\epsilon[C\mid i]=0\),
\[
\widetilde G(i,\epsilon)=G(i,\epsilon)-a_\psi(i)^\top C(i,\epsilon)
\]
retains the conditional mean for fixed or appropriately trained coefficients \(a_\psi\). Learning the mapping across data points amortizes the effort of choosing controls.

**What changes.** **Control coefficients and gradient estimation**, not the VI objective.

The recognition network shares information about useful controls across observations, avoiding a separate optimization problem for each new minibatch. Centering the control preserves the desired gradient; its coefficient must not acquire an unaccounted dependence on the same random draw. It mainly attacks Monte Carlo noise conditional on the data, so substantial minibatch noise can remain.
