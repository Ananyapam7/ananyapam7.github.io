Semi-implicit inference combines an implicit mixing distribution with a tractable conditional density. The hard part is its marginal score, which appears when differentiating reverse KL through a sampled value.

Let \(z=T_\phi(\epsilon)\), with fixed base noise \(\epsilon\), and let \(J_\phi T_\phi\) be the sampler Jacobian. The path-gradient identity is
\[
\nabla_\phi\mathrm{KL}(q_\phi\|\pi)
=\mathbb E_\epsilon\!\left[(J_\phi T_\phi)^\top\{\nabla_z\log q_\phi(z)-\nabla_z\log\gamma(z)\}\right].
\]
The unknown normalizer disappears, but the marginal score remains. This paper uses kernel smoothing and the tractable conditional scores of the hierarchy to construct a **kernelized path gradient**. Its importance-sampling extension learns a proposal for auxiliary variables to improve the bias–variance tradeoff.

**What changes.** Unlike Kernel SIVI's KSD criterion, this route starts from KL path gradients and a kernel-smoothed construction. The paper connects it to amortized SVGD and establishes a lower-variance semi-implicit estimator under the analyzed setup. Smoothing introduces bias; the importance correction reduces that difficulty rather than making every finite-sample gradient exact.

It is useful when conditional scores and reparameterized draws are available but marginal densities are not. Kernel bandwidth, auxiliary proposal quality, and computational cost still determine whether the lower-noise direction helps in practice. This entry uses the published AISTATS 2026 version.
