Particle methods represent an approximation by moving a collection of points. But a finite empirical measure has no ordinary density, so inserting it directly into continuous reverse KL is not a well-defined finite objective.

This paper explains the smoothing that makes finite-particle VI possible and relates two forms of that approximation. The continuous ideal is a distributional gradient flow,
\[
\partial_t q_t+\nabla\!\cdot(q_t v_t)=0,
\qquad v_t=\nabla\log\pi-\nabla\log q_t
\]
for Wasserstein steepest descent of reverse KL. The target term attracts mass; the density term prevents collapse. Finite-particle algorithms must approximate this evolution through smoothing of densities or the relevant function space.

**What changes.** The paper uses this geometric account to build an acceleration framework and a principled bandwidth-selection method for particle VI. Bandwidth controls what the particle cloud can resolve, rather than merely changing a plotting appearance. Too much smoothing can merge distinct structure; too little can leave poor interactions and unstable estimates. Acceleration improves dynamics but does not remove finite-particle error.
