**The obstacle.** Why should greedy addition of mixture components improve an objective?

**Mathematical move.** Treat distributions as points in a convex space and the KL/negative ELBO as a functional. From current \(q^{(t)}\), choose a component \(r\) that approximately minimizes a *linearization* \(\langle\nabla\mathcal J(q^{(t)}),r\rangle\), then set \(q^{(t+1)}=(1-\eta_t)q^{(t)}+\eta_t r_t\). This is a functional Frank–Wolfe or conditional-gradient viewpoint. For reverse KL, the first variation of \(\mathcal J(q)=\int q\log(q/\pi)\) is \(\log(q/\pi)+1\).

**Reusable insight.** Growing a mixture can be viewed as optimization over the convex hull of a simpler base family. This yields a route to progress guarantees under suitable conditions and explains the component-addition strategy of boosting VI.

At iteration \(t\), write the mixture update consistently as
\[
q^{(t+1)}=(1-\eta_t)q^{(t)}+\eta_t r_t.
\]
The new component is directed toward regions where the current approximation underrepresents the target. The guarantees require control of the objective's curvature and the component-search error. A local component optimizer can miss the useful direction, and every added component increases evaluation cost.
