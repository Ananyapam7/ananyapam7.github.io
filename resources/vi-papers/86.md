**The obstacle.** Ordinary neural-network training estimates one set of weights but does not represent uncertainty about them.

**Mathematical move.** Treat network weights \(W\) as the Bayesian unknown \(z\), choose a tractable \(q_\phi(W)\), and maximize
\[
\mathcal L(\phi)=\mathbb E_{q_\phi(W)}\log p(x\mid W)-\mathrm{KL}(q_\phi(W)\|p(W)).
\]
For Gaussian weights, draw \(W=m+s\odot\epsilon\) and backpropagate through the stochastic weights. Unlike a VAE, the primary Bayesian unknown here is the **network weight vector**, not a per-observation latent code.

**What changes.** A Bayesian model of network weights plus a practical gradient estimator. The predictive distribution averages \(p(x_{\rm new}\mid W)\) over \(q_\phi(W)\).

Here \(W\) is simply the collection called \(z\) in the general setup; \(\phi=(m,s)\) controls its approximation. One sampled set of weights serves an entire forward pass. A mean-field Gaussian can still miss strong weight correlations and distinct equivalent network solutions, so a Bayesian training objective alone does not guarantee accurate uncertainty.
