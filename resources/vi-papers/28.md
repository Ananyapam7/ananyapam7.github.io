IWAE changes how many importance samples are combined. Another, independent lever is what discrepancies between the approximation and target we care about. Reverse KL does not penalize missed target mass in the same way as divergences with stronger density-ratio weighting. Rényi VI varies that weighting through a continuous order parameter.

Replace the ELBO by

\[
\mathcal L_\alpha=\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\log\mathbb E_q[w^{1-\alpha}]
=\log Z-D_\alpha(q\|\pi).
\]

For \(\alpha\to1\), expanding \(\log\mathbb E[\exp((1-\alpha)\log w)]\) to first order recovers \(\mathbb E[\log w]\). Changing \(\alpha\) changes the power of the importance weights and the divergence being optimized. [IWAE](#paper-27) changes the number of samples *inside* a Monte Carlo bound. This paper changes the underlying divergence. [VIMCO-star](#paper-50) studies an objective that combines the two constructions.

**The divergence determines which mistakes are expensive.** The ELBO averages log weights, which moderates the influence of an exceptionally large ratio. A positive power of the weight gives more influence to a draw where the target is large relative to the approximation. Varying \(\alpha\) changes this emphasis and therefore changes which member of a restricted family is preferred.

The link to the ELBO is especially transparent if \(X=\log w\) and \(a=1-\alpha\). Near \(a=0\), when the relevant moments exist,

\[
\frac1a\log\mathbb E[e^{aX}]
=\mathbb E[X]+\frac a2\operatorname{Var}(X)+O(a^2).
\]

The first term is the ELBO; the next term shows how moving away from \(\alpha=1\) changes sensitivity to variation in the log weights. This is a change in the objective's preferences, not a control variate for the old objective.

**An important practical distinction.** At \(\alpha=0\), the exact expression is \(\log\mathbb E_q[w]=\log Z\), provided \(q\) covers the target. It then contains no inference signal. A finite-sample log average has a different expectation and can still train \(q\), connecting the construction to IWAE. Large powered weights can make optimization unstable, and the sign and bound interpretation depend on the chosen order. A value of \(\alpha\) should therefore be chosen for the desired discrepancy and the estimator one can actually compute.

**What carries forward.** An objective change can alter which approximation is optimal even when the model, family, and optimizer are fixed. A finite-sample estimator of the new objective introduces a separate statistical question that should not be confused with the population divergence.
