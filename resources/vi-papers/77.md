A mixture can grow one component at a time, but its next component needs a useful training objective. Fitting the original target again may simply rediscover the mode already represented.

The paper introduces the **residual ELBO**. Given the current mixture \(q^{(t)}\), fit a component \(r\) using
\[
\operatorname{RELBO}_t(r)=\mathbb E_r[\log\gamma(z)-\log q^{(t)}(z)]+\lambda H(r),\qquad \lambda>0.
\]
The residual term rewards regions where the target is large relative to the current approximation. The entropy term discourages a degenerate point-like component. An ordinary black-box VI solver can optimize this modified objective, then the mixture becomes
\[
q^{(t+1)}=(1-\eta_t)q^{(t)}+\eta_t r_t.
\]
**What changes.** The contribution makes the conditional-gradient view of boosting computationally usable, with relaxed curvature assumptions and a stopping criterion based on the optimization duality gap. It changes component fitting, not the Bayesian target.

A symmetric residual can still leave the inner optimizer stuck. Once the main distribution is well fitted, extra components may chase tails at considerable cost. The stopping criterion and the quality of the component solver therefore matter.
