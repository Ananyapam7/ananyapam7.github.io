Sigmoid belief networks are directed models of binary variables. Each unit's probability depends nonlinearly on its parents, so computing the likelihood of observed units can require summing over exponentially many hidden configurations. Saul, Jaakkola, and Jordan develop a tractable mean-field lower bound for these networks.

For hidden units \(z_j\in\{0,1\}\), use Bernoulli factors

\[
q_\phi(z)=\prod_j m_j^{z_j}(1-m_j)^{1-z_j},
\qquad \phi=(m_1,\ldots,m_J).
\]

**Optimize beliefs one at a time.** Holding the other factors fixed turns a joint inference problem into a sequence of smaller updates. The general exact coordinate-ascent update is

Write \(q_{-j}=\prod_{k\ne j}q_k\). The core update is

\[
\boxed{
q_j^{(t+1)}(z_j)\propto
\exp\!\left(\mathbb E_{q_{-j}^{(t)}}[\log\gamma(z)]\right).
}
\]

For each possible value of \(z_j\), average the log target over our current uncertainty about the other blocks. Exponentiate and normalize to obtain the updated factor. We average over the other distributions rather than fixing their unknowns at single values.

**Why it works.** This is the exact maximizing factor when the displayed distribution is available in the chosen family. With every other factor fixed, it cannot lower the ELBO:

\[
\mathcal L[q^{(t+1)}]\geq\mathcal L[q^{(t)}].
\]

Here \(t\) counts individual factor updates. A complete pass through the factors is a sweep. Each step replaces a whole distribution, including its spread.

**The paper's additional step.** In a sigmoid network, an input \(a_i=b_i+\sum_jW_{ij}z_j\) produces a log-normalization term \(\log(1+e^{a_i})\). Its expectation under independent Bernoulli variables is still difficult. The authors introduce auxiliary variational parameters to obtain a tractable lower bound, which can be optimized over the means and the auxiliary parameters. Ascent applies to that additional bound; the exact update above explains the coordinate principle but is not, by itself, an implementable sigmoid-network algorithm.

**A concrete illustration of coordinate ascent.** For the correlated Gaussian in the [mean-field paper entry](#method-mfvi), the updates alternate as

\[
q_1^{(t+1)}=\mathcal N(\rho m_2^{(t)},1-\rho^2),
\qquad
q_2^{(t+2)}=\mathcal N(\rho m_1^{(t+1)},1-\rho^2).
\]

**Where it struggles.** The expectation and normalization must be tractable; conditional conjugacy often makes this possible. Otherwise, [BBVI](#paper-3) offers estimated gradients. Strong coupling can slow the alternating updates, and general objectives can have poor local optima. Even perfect convergence cannot recover dependence excluded by mean field. [SVI](#paper-2) addresses the separate cost of processing a large dataset per update.

**What carries forward.** This paper extends mean-field inference to nonlinear directed networks through a tractable bound. The transferable optimization pattern is coordinate ascent. [VMP](#paper-51) makes exact tractable updates reusable; [nonconjugate VI](#paper-53) and [stochastic search](#paper-66) offer other ways to handle the expectations that block them.
