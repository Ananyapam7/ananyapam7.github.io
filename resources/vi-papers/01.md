Mean field leaves us with several factors to choose. Why should that make optimization easier? Because when all but one factor are fixed, the ELBO can be reorganized into a KL divergence involving only that factor. We can solve an optimization over a whole distribution in one step—without differentiating a long vector of parameters.

Write \(q_{-j}=\prod_{k\ne j}q_k\). The core update is

\[
\boxed{
q_j^{(t+1)}(z_j)\propto
\exp\!\left(\mathbb E_{q_{-j}^{(t)}}[\log\gamma(z)]\right).
}
\]

For each possible value of \(z_j\), average the log target over our current uncertainty about the other blocks. Exponentiate and normalize to obtain the updated factor. We average over the other distributions rather than fixing their unknowns at single values.

**Why it works.** This is the exact maximizing factor when the displayed distribution is available in the chosen family. With every other factor fixed, it cannot lower the ELBO:

\[
\mathcal L[q^{(t+1)}]\geq\mathcal L[q^{(t)}].
\]

Here \(t\) counts individual factor updates. A complete pass through the factors is a sweep. Each step replaces a whole distribution, including its spread.

**A concrete example.** For the correlated Gaussian in the MFVI section, the updates alternate as

\[
q_1^{(t+1)}=\mathcal N(\rho m_2^{(t)},1-\rho^2),
\qquad
q_2^{(t+2)}=\mathcal N(\rho m_1^{(t+1)},1-\rho^2).
\]

Each mean follows the current mean of the other factor. The second update uses the first factor's newly updated mean. Watch one marginal move while the other stays fixed:

**Where it struggles.** The expectation and normalization must be tractable; conditional conjugacy often makes this possible. Otherwise, [BBVI](#paper-3) offers estimated gradients. Strong coupling can slow the alternating updates, and general objectives can have poor local optima. Even perfect convergence cannot recover dependence excluded by mean field. [SVI](#paper-2) addresses the separate cost of processing a large dataset per update.

CAVI is an optimization strategy for a factorized approximation: it changes which factor is optimized at each step, while keeping the target and ELBO fixed. The featured Saul–Jaakkola–Jordan paper applies bound ascent to sigmoid belief networks, using Bernoulli mean-field factors and auxiliary bounding parameters to handle their nonlinear conditionals. The general update below describes exact coordinate maximization; that paper's extra bound is needed when the model does not make this update tractable.

**What carries forward.** CAVI is a *functional coordinate optimizer*, not a special likelihood or a model of its own. Whenever the expected log target can be normalized in a tractable form, the same argument gives an exact factor update. Otherwise the identity is still valid but not directly implementable.
