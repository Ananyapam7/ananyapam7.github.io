**The obstacle.** Message passing is elegant for conjugate factors, but categorical/logistic observations create non-Gaussian local messages.

**Mathematical move.** Instead of demanding closed-form conjugate messages, approximate a difficult variational factor by a tractable exponential-family distribution, then optimize its parameters using derivatives of the expected log factor. For a Gaussian factor \(q(z)=\mathcal N(m,\Sigma)\), the identity \(\nabla_m\mathbb E_q f(z)=\mathbb E_q\nabla_z f(z)\) (when justified) illustrates how a nonconjugate expected log likelihood can enter an iterative update. The paper develops practical fixed-point/message rules for binary and multinomial regression rather than a universal plug-in Gaussian update.

**Reusable insight.** A graphical model can remain local even when its messages require numerical approximation. Separate **locality of information** from **analytic tractability of messages**.

The paper also develops a softmax bound for multinomial regression. Its message calculations can use approximations or quadrature for difficult expectations. The Gaussian derivative identity explains how local information enters; it is not itself a universally valid coordinate-ascent update. Approximation quality and fixed-point stability matter, especially when the posterior is far from the chosen exponential family.
