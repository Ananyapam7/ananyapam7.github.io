An inference calculation may be hard because a single local term couples many variables. This foundational treatment shows how variational bounds can replace such terms with tractable expressions and thereby simplify the graph.

**Trade a difficult function for an adjustable bound.** A concave function \(f\) lies below its tangent at \(\xi\):

\[
f(u)\leq f(\xi)+f'(\xi)(u-\xi).
\]

If \(u=\sum_j a_jz_j\), the right-hand side is additive in the variables. Exponentiating it produces a product of separate terms. The new parameter \(\xi\) lets us choose where the approximation is tight.

For example, the log sigmoid \(f(u)=\log\sigma(u)\) is concave. Its tangent gives an upper bound on the sigmoid after exponentiation. This is useful for understanding how a nonlinear coupling can become separable. The direction of the bound matters: that particular construction is an upper bound, not automatically an ELBO.

The broader idea is convex duality: represent a function as the best member of a family of simpler functions. A chosen member is tractable; optimizing its variational parameter tightens the approximation.

**Simplifying the approximation's graph is another choice.** A structured family might retain a chain,

\[
q_\phi(z)\propto
\prod_j r_j(z_j)\prod_j r_{j,j+1}(z_j,z_{j+1}),
\]

while omitting harder long-range interactions. Exact inference inside this simpler chain can remain efficient even though the original model is difficult.

Imagine neighboring latent states in a time series. Fully independent factors cannot represent a tendency for consecutive states to agree. Keeping the chain preserves that behavior while sacrificing some other dependencies. The price is more work per variational update.

**What this reading adds.** This is a tutorial and synthesis, not a single new optimizer. It connects local functional bounds, tractable graphical structures, and variational optimization. These are different ways to make an inference computation manageable.

Loose bounds can distort the fit, and a simplified graph can omit important dependence. [VMP](#paper-51) turns suitable local calculations into reusable messages; [nonconjugate VI](#paper-53) uses local approximations when exact expectations fail. [Stochastic search](#paper-66) shows how an approximation can instead serve as a variance-reduction tool without replacing the intended objective.
