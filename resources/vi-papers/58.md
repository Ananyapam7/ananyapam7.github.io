[BBVI](#paper-3) usually estimates its gradient with draws from the distribution it is fitting. These need not be the best draws for estimating that particular integral.

Write the score-gradient integrand as
\[
g_\phi(z)=S_\phi(z)\,[\log\gamma(z)-\log q_\phi(z)],
\qquad S_\phi(z)=\nabla_\phi\log q_\phi(z).
\]
For a proposal \(r\) covering the relevant support,
\[
\nabla_\phi\mathcal L
=\mathbb E_r\!\left[\frac{q_\phi(z)}{r(z)}g_\phi(z)\right].
\]

**Change where we look, then compensate for looking there more often.** If rare tail draws produce large gradient contributions, ordinary sampling may alternate between missing them and receiving an enormous update. A more dispersed proposal visits those regions more often; the weight \(q_\phi/r\) compensates.

For gradient coordinate \(j\), the variance-minimizing proposal has the form
\[
r_j^\star(z)\propto q_\phi(z)|g_{\phi,j}(z)|.
\]
This follows by minimizing the second moment \(\int q_\phi(z)^2g_{\phi,j}(z)^2/r(z)\,dz\). Sampling should reflect the magnitude of the contribution to the integral, not just the density \(q_\phi\).

The ideal proposal is generally intractable. Overdispersed BBVI uses an exponential-family approximation and proposals from its overdispersed counterpart, adapting dispersion to reduce estimated variance. It combines this with Rao–Blackwellization, control variates, and multiple proposals.

For a Gaussian example, \(q_\phi=\mathcal N(m,s^2)\) can remain fixed while the gradient proposal is \(r=\mathcal N(m,\tau s^2)\), with \(\tau>1\). The approximation has not become more uncertain: only the numerical sampling rule has widened.

**What changes.** The target, variational family, and ELBO are retained. The exponential-family assumption here organizes the **variational proposal construction**; it is not a conjugacy requirement on the model as in SVI.

Too much dispersion wastes draws where contributions are tiny. High-dimensional importance weights can also become unstable, which motivates the paper's factorwise treatment. This is complementary to [control variates](#paper-24): one changes the sampling law, the other changes the integrand while preserving its expectation.
