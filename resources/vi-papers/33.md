Rainforth et al. identify a problem with the standard inference gradient of IWAE. Is the objective responsible, or are we using an inefficient estimator? DReG keeps exactly the same multisample objective and reexpresses the weighted score contribution through a second pathwise identity.

Use \(z^{(s)}=T_\phi(\epsilon^{(s)})\), \(w_s=w_\phi(z^{(s)})\), and \(\bar w_s=w_s/\sum_{r=1}^K w_r\).

In a reparameterized IWAE gradient, the explicit score term is multiplied by the random normalized weight \(\bar w_s\). This product generally has nonzero expectation, so the deletion used in [Sticking the Landing](#paper-22) no longer applies. DReG instead rewrites the expectation of that score term as a pathwise derivative, yielding

\[
\nabla_\phi\mathcal L_K
=\mathbb E\!\left[\sum_{s=1}^K\bar w_s^2\,
\nabla_{z^{(s)}}\log w_s\,\partial_\phi z^{(s)}\right].
\]

The squared normalized weights are the visible result of the second reparameterization. At \(K=1\), \(\bar w_1=1\) and the formula reduces to [Sticking the Landing](#paper-22). [VIMCO-star](#paper-50) pursues an analogous SNR improvement for cases where a pathwise derivative is unavailable.

**The weighted score is the obstacle.** An unweighted score averages to zero, but a sample with a large importance weight is not a typical unweighted draw. Multiplying its score by that weight changes the expectation. DReG applies a second reparameterization identity to account for this dependence, and combines the resulting terms rather than throwing one away.

The squared weights give an intuition for the scaling. If the weights are reasonably balanced, \(\bar w_s\) is approximately \(1/K\), so the \(K\) terms each carry a coefficient of about \(1/K^2\). The mean inference signal is still small, but the estimator now cancels more of the noise that obscured it. Under the paper's asymptotic assumptions, its inference-gradient SNR improves as \(\sqrt K\).

At an exact posterior fit, every \(w_s=Z\) and every spatial derivative \(\nabla_z\log w_s=0\). The estimator is then zero for each sampled set, extending STL's pointwise cancellation.

**When the construction applies.** It needs differentiable reparameterized draws and the appropriate moments for the SNR analysis. Discrete latents require another route, such as [VIMCO-star](#paper-50). A single dominating weight also limits the benefit of averaging. In code, the displayed formula specifies an estimator directly; blindly differentiating a surrogate with squared weights can introduce unwanted derivatives unless its weights and density parameters are held fixed in the intended places.

**What carries forward.** This is a clean example of improving optimization without changing the target, family, or objective. The squared normalized weights reflect a valid derivative identity, not a heuristic deletion of terms as in a naive multisample STL.
