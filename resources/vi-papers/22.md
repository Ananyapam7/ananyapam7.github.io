Variance reduction is not only a discrete-variable problem. Even for a smooth Gaussian draw, the ordinary pathwise ELBO derivative contains a term whose expectation is exactly zero. Sticking the Landing removes it, revealing that at an exact posterior fit the remaining per-sample gradient can be identically zero.

Write \(z_\phi=T_\phi(\epsilon)\), with \(\epsilon\sim\nu\). Expand the total derivative in [AEVB](#paper-4):

\[
\frac{d}{d\phi}\log w_\phi(z_\phi)
=\nabla_z\log w_\phi(z_\phi)\,\partial_\phi z_\phi
-\left.\partial_\phi\log q_\phi(z)\right|_{z=z_\phi}.
\]

The second term is a zero-mean score. Drop it and keep the first. If \(q_\phi=\pi\), then \(\log w_\phi(z)=\log Z\) is constant in \(z\), so the remaining estimator vanishes for every draw. This gives the zero-variance property at an exact posterior fit. In [BBVI](#paper-3) the score helps *construct* the gradient. Here it is a dispensable term in a pathwise gradient. The same deletion needs extra care for [IWAE](#paper-27)’s multisample objective, which motivates [DReG](#paper-33).

**An exact fit should stop producing random pushes.** Take a standard normal target and \(q_\phi=\mathcal N(m,s^2)\), with \(z=m+s\epsilon\). For the derivative with respect to \(m\), the ordinary total derivative and STL estimator are

\[
\widehat g_m^{\mathrm{total}}=-m-s\epsilon,
\qquad
\widehat g_m^{\mathrm{STL}}=-m+(s^{-1}-s)\epsilon.
\]

Both have mean \(-m\). When \(s=1\), the STL estimator is exactly \(-m\) for every draw; at \(m=0\), it is identically zero. The total derivative still contains the random term \(-\epsilon\), even though the posterior fit is exact.

**The benefit depends on the fit.** The same example shows why removal of a zero-mean term does not always reduce variance: if \(s\) is very small, \(s^{-1}-s\) can be large. The zero-variance property is particularly compelling near a well-specified optimum. If the family cannot represent the posterior, residual noise can remain, a distinction developed in [STL convergence](#paper-47).

In an autodiff implementation, the explicit parameters inside the log-density evaluation are held fixed for the score deletion, while differentiation through the sampled \(z\) is preserved. Stopping the entire log-density derivative would remove a necessary part of the pathwise term as well.

**What carries forward.** This result is an estimator identity, not a new ELBO or family. Crucially, a zero-mean term need not always be beneficial to remove away from the optimum. The strong claim is pointwise cancellation at a well-specified fit, which later convergence papers exploit.
