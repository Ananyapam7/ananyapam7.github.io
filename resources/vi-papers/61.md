A diagnostic can warn that a fit looks unreliable. A stronger question is how far its reported mean or uncertainty could be from the posterior value. Validated VI connects computable variational quantities to bounds on those errors.

The key intermediate quantity is Wasserstein distance: the smallest average distance needed to transport one distribution into another. Unlike KL, it retains the units and scale of \(z\). For any function \(h\) with Lipschitz constant \(L_h\),

\[
|\mathbb E_\pi[h]-\mathbb E_{q_\phi}[h]|
\leq L_h W_1(\pi,q_\phi).
\]

In particular, controlling transport distance controls the mean error. The paper also gives bounds for uncertainty summaries.

**Bracket the normalizer to control a divergence.** Define the order-two upper bound

\[
\mathcal U_2(q_\phi)=\tfrac12
\log\mathbb E_{q_\phi}\!\left[\left(\frac{\gamma}{q_\phi}\right)^2\right].
\]

For any reference approximation \(q_{\mathrm{ref}}\), its ELBO is a lower bound on \(\log Z\). Consequently,

\[
D_2(\pi\|q_\phi)
=2[\mathcal U_2(q_\phi)-\log Z]
\leq \Delta,\qquad
\Delta=2[\mathcal U_2(q_\phi)-\mathcal L[q_{\mathrm{ref}}]].
\]

The upper and lower bounds may come from different fitted approximations. This connects directly to [CHIVI](#paper-29).

To turn this scale-free discrepancy into a distance, the paper adds moment information. One of its bounds, for \(p\geq1\), is

\[
W_p(\pi,q_\phi)\leq
C_{2p}(q_\phi)(e^\Delta-1)^{1/(2p)},\qquad
C_{2p}(q_\phi)=2\inf_a
\left(\mathbb E_{q_\phi}\|z-a\|^{2p}\right)^{1/(2p)}.
\]

The approximation must cover the target's support, and the displayed quantities must be finite. The moment factor supplies scale: multiplying \(z\) by ten leaves a density-ratio divergence unchanged but multiplies distances and mean errors by ten.

**What changes.** This is an accuracy-analysis workflow, not a new generic optimizer. It supplements [PSIS diagnostics](#paper-54) with bounds tied to posterior summaries.

Heavy importance-weight tails can make the upper bound infinite or hard to estimate. Conservative bounds may be loose. A Monte Carlo plug-in value is an estimate of a bound; certified numerical statements additionally need control of estimation error. These limitations identify when the available computation cannot yet support a strong accuracy claim.
