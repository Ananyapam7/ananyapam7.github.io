A mode-finding optimizer visits many locations before it stops. Pathfinder asks whether those intermediate locations already contain enough geometric information to build useful posterior approximations.

Run L-BFGS on \(-\log\gamma(z)\). At iteration \(t\), let \(a^{(t)}\) denote the current location and \(\Sigma^{(t)}\) a positive inverse-curvature estimate. A local quadratic approximation suggests

\[
q^{(t)}(z)=\mathcal N(z\mid m^{(t)},\Sigma^{(t)}),
\qquad
m^{(t)}=a^{(t)}
+\Sigma^{(t)}\nabla_z\log\gamma(a^{(t)}).
\]

The gradient shifts the center toward the maximum of that local quadratic. L-BFGS supplies curvature information from a short history of position and gradient changes, avoiding a full Hessian calculation.

**Choose the approximation, not merely the highest-density point.** Draw samples from each Gaussian candidate and estimate its ELBO:

\[
\widehat{\mathcal L}^{(t)}
=\frac1K\sum_{s=1}^K
\left[\log\gamma(z^{(t,s)})-\log q^{(t)}(z^{(t,s)})\right],
\qquad z^{(t,s)}\sim q^{(t)}.
\]

Return the candidate with the largest estimated value. The final optimization iterate need not win: its curvature may describe a narrow peak less usefully than an earlier candidate describes the posterior's typical region.

For a Gaussian target with covariance \(\Sigma\), exact curvature makes the mean correction recover the target mean from any location. For a non-Gaussian target, different locations give different local Gaussian descriptions, so selection matters.

Multiple independent paths can explore different regions. The multi-path method combines their candidates using importance resampling and PSIS, improving robustness to unlucky starts.

**What changes.** Pathfinder constructs and selects approximations using an optimization trajectory. It does not run stochastic ELBO gradients over a Gaussian family's parameters as [ADVI](#paper-6) does. The ELBO is used to compare candidates.

It is useful for fast approximate draws and initializations for more expensive inference. A Gaussian candidate can still miss curved geometry or heavy tails; all paths can miss a remote mode. Importance resampling cannot recover regions absent from the proposals, and noisy ELBO comparisons can choose the wrong candidate. [Diagnostics](#paper-54) remain relevant after the fast fit.
