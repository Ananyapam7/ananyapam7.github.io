A fully Bayesian neural network places uncertainty over every weight. A Bayesian last layer instead learns features \(h_\theta(x)\) and places a variational distribution over the final linear map. This paper makes that compromise especially cheap by deriving **deterministic, sampling-free variational training objectives**.

For scalar Gaussian regression, take \(q_\phi(w)=\mathcal N(m,\Sigma)\), prediction \(w^\top h\), and observation noise variance \(\sigma^2\). The expected squared error is exactly
\[
\mathbb E_{q_\phi}(y-w^\top h)^2=(y-m^\top h)^2+h^\top\Sigma h.
\]
Therefore the expected log likelihood is
\[
-\tfrac12\log(2\pi\sigma^2)-\frac{(y-m^\top h)^2+h^\top\Sigma h}{2\sigma^2}.
\]
Sum these terms and subtract the Gaussian weight KL: there is no need to sample final-layer weights. The additional variance term teaches the features and weight distribution to account for uncertainty rather than only mean prediction. Classification uses tractable variational bounds rather than this Gaussian regression identity.

**What changes.** The contribution is more than restricting uncertainty to the final layer: analytic expectations and bounds enable single-pass training and evaluation, with quadratic cost in last-layer width. The paper also combines this with Bayesian feature learning, analytically removing a source of weight-sampling noise.

For deterministic features, uncertainty about the feature extractor remains unrepresented. A confidently wrong representation can therefore still yield misleading predictions. Learning \(\theta\) alongside \(\phi\) does not make those feature parameters Bayesian.
