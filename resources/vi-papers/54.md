A flat ELBO trace tells us that optimization has stopped making progress. It does not tell us whether the fitted distribution gives useful uncertainty estimates. This paper adds two ways to inspect the result.

**First, ask whether draws from the approximation can stand in for draws from the posterior.** For \(z^{(s)}\sim q_\phi\), calculate

\[
w_s=\frac{\gamma(z^{(s)})}{q_\phi(z^{(s)})},
\qquad
\mathbb E_\pi[h(z)]\approx
\sum_{s=1}^K\bar w_s h(z^{(s)}).
\]

The unknown normalizing constant cancels in \(\bar w_s=w_s/\sum_r w_r\). If a few weights dominate, rare draws are doing almost all the work: the approximation undersamples important parts of the target.

Pareto-smoothed importance sampling (PSIS) stabilizes the largest weights by fitting their tail. Its estimated tail-shape parameter \(\widehat k\) diagnoses the difficulty of importance sampling. Larger values indicate more unstable weights; the paper uses \(0.7\) as a practical warning threshold.

For a simple illustration, let \(\pi=\mathcal N(0,1)\) and \(q_\phi=\mathcal N(0,s^2)\). Then

\[
\frac{\pi(z)}{q_\phi(z)}
=s\exp\!\left\{\tfrac12(s^{-2}-1)z^2\right\}.
\]

When \(s<1\), the ratio grows in the tails. A narrow approximation can look convincing near the peak while giving too little probability to extreme values.

**Second, repeat inference where the latent truth is known.** Variational simulation-based calibration draws a latent state and dataset from the model, fits VI, and records where the generated state falls in each variational marginal. Across simulated datasets, systematic asymmetry in these probabilities can expose biased central estimates. This evaluates the inference procedure across model-generated problems, rather than only the observed dataset.

**What changes.** These are diagnostics and optional importance corrections after fitting. They do not change the ELBO. A small estimated tail parameter is not a universal accuracy certificate: finite samples may miss an entire mode, and calibration averaged over simulated datasets does not certify every individual fit. [Validated VI](#paper-61) asks the next question: can we bound errors in particular posterior summaries?
