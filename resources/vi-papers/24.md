Suppose we already have several useful controls: one from Taylor expansion, another from an alternative gradient estimator, perhaps another from a known identity. We shouldn't have to choose only one. But fitting their combination introduces a new estimation problem, because the covariance matrix of the controls is itself noisy.

Collect the zero-mean control quantities into a vector \(C\). For the corrected gradient \(\widehat g_\phi-a^\top C\), the variance-minimizing coefficient vector for a scalar gradient component solves \(\operatorname{Cov}(C,C)a=\operatorname{Cov}(C,\widehat g_\phi)\), when the covariance is invertible. With many controls and few draws, estimating that system is itself noisy. The paper studies which controls can be combined and how to estimate their coefficients using regularization and prior information. Its subject is the **combination rule**. [Taylor controls](#paper-23) and [Learned quadratic controls](#paper-25) primarily design individual controls.

**Different estimators can become controls for one another.** Suppose \(g_A\) and \(g_B\) estimate the same gradient. Their difference has zero mean, so

\[
\widehat g=g_A-a(g_A-g_B)
\]

is still unbiased for a fixed coefficient \(a\). This lets us combine an analytic term with its sampled estimate, two different reparameterizations, or several approximation-based controls. The paper treats these constructions as parts of one ensemble rather than making a separate choice for every model term.

The optimal coefficients resemble a multiple regression: predict the fluctuations of the base gradient using the controls, then subtract that prediction. Two almost-identical controls add little information, even if both individually correlate with the gradient. Their covariance matrix captures that redundancy.

**Why estimating the coefficients is part of the problem.** With only a few draws, an unregularized covariance estimate can be nearly singular and produce huge coefficients. A control that looks excellent on those draws may behave poorly on the next batch. The paper uses regularization and prior information to stabilize this estimation. Coefficients should be learned from information separate from the fresh corrected draw when relying on the fixed-coefficient unbiasedness argument. More controls help only when their extra predictive value outweighs estimation noise and computation.

**What carries forward.** Combining control variates is a regression problem over random gradient fluctuations. The coefficients can be portable across estimator families, but their estimation and possible dependence on the same Monte Carlo draws must be treated carefully.
